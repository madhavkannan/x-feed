Shakeout or Breakdown? Today's gap down is a live lesson.

One of the most important moments in a market correction is when the major indexes approach their 200-day SMA.

This is where we often see two very different outcomes: a temporary shakeout that eventually leads to a recovery, or a sustained breakdown that develops into a deeper correction.

And the difference between these two scenarios can have a significant impact on how we manage risk and deploy capital.

Here's where we stand today:
- $QQQ: Down 4%, trading near $685 and approaching its 200-day SMA around $680.
- $SMH: Down 8%, trading near $514 and already below its 200-day SMA around $519.
- $VIX: Near 22, up 28% today, but still below its recent spike high around 26.

A decline toward the 200-day SMA isn't automatically a sell signal. What matters is how the market responds.

Shakeout: (This is what I call a fast death)
- Price briefly undercuts the 200-day SMA and reclaims it.
- Leading stocks hold their key support levels.
- Selling pressure fades, followed by signs of renewed institutional buying.

Breakdown: (This is what I call a slow death)
- Price loses the 200-day SMA and struggles to reclaim it.
- Relief rallies get rejected near resistance.
- Lower highs and lower lows develop, accompanied by heavy selling volume.

Gap-down day playbook:
- Don't make emotional decisions at the open.
- Avoid rushing to buy on Day 1. Give the selling pressure time to settle.
- Respect your exit rules when leaders break down.
- Watch which stocks defend or reclaim their 200-day SMA.
- Look for a confirmed follow-through day before increasing exposure. (this is very important)

Corrections can eventually create the next generation of market leaders, but not every dip is an opportunity.

The difference between a temporary shakeout and a sustained breakdown often becomes clearer with time, price action, and volume.

A shakeout can create opportunities, while a sustained breakdown can lead to deeper losses.

The goal isn't to predict the exact bottom, but to understand the risks, remain disciplined, and recognize when market conditions begin to improve.
