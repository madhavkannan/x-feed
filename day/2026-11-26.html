I've been thinking a lot about the similarities between fighting inflation in 2022 and in 2026, and the more I dig, the more it feels like history rhymes.

In 2022, CPI hit 9.1%, the Fed hiked from 0.25% to 4.50% in nine months, and the DXY ripped from 98 to 114.8.

In 2026, CPI peaked at 4.2% in May on the Iran energy shock. The Fed has hiked once, 25 bp in September, and the DXY has gone from 99.9 to 103.4.

In 2022 the dollar topped in late September, the S&P bottomed two weeks later, and the Fed kept hiking until July 2023. The dollar topped when the market stopped adding hikes, nine months before the Fed actually stopped.

Inflation is about to look worse before it looks better, because late 2025 is a low base after the shutdown, so November through February should print around 4% a year. That's the window where the market prices hikes in December, January and April and the dollar makes its last push.

This year's war spike, 0.9% in March and 0.6% in April, drops out of the yearly numbers in the spring. At 0.3% a month, CPI falls from about 4.3% to 3.2% by May, and at 0.2% it lands near 2.5%.

My base case is that the dollar tops between February and April 2027, somewhere around 105 to 106 on the DXY. That's about a third of the 2022 move, which fits a cycle with half the inflation and a fraction of the hikes, and 2022's pace from first hike to top also lands in early April.

What breaks it is inflation running at 0.4% a month or oil spiking again in the spring. Then the base effect never shows up and the dollar stays higher for longer.

When the dollar tops, the pressure comes off the semis and AI names that got hit this month. The market is trading this like 2022, but the setup looks a lot more like the moment 2022 turned.

History rhymes. But this time it looks like a shorter tune.
