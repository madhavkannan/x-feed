What does this mean for the stock market?

Healthcare:
- healthcare is the largest category on this list. It is literally the future. Huge aging population
- the system also faces a binding capacity constraint. Physician shortages (especially primary care) are projected to persist deep into the 2030s
- home health aides, therapy assistants, and health services managers are scaling to support aging-in-place and higher-acuity outpatient care
- mental health and substance-use roles also reflect a multi-year crisis
- Stocks for advanced practice and managed care: $UNH, $ELV, $HUM, $CI
- Stocks for devices: $ISRG, $SYK, $MDT, $BSX
- Age-related: $LLY, $ABBV, $MRK, $AMGN, $NVO
- Senior housing: $WELL, $VTR

Technology:
- data volume is compounding across every industry (including healthcare records, imaging, and claims)
- data scientists exist to turn that volume into usable signals
- AI requires continuous research, not just implementation.
- computer and information research scientists invent the next algorithms and systems as basic data science becomes more widespread
- connectivity permanently expands the attack surface - Information security analysts grow because the cost of being unsecured keeps rising; especially in healthcare, finance, and critical infrastructure
- stocks for AI infrastructure & platforms: $NVDA, $MSFT, $GOOGL, $AMZN, $AVGO, $AMD, $SNOW, $PLTR
- stocks for cybersecurity: $CRWD, $PANW, $ZS, $FTNT, $S, $OKTA

Renewables:
- solar and wind appear because of the energy transition, but the absolute job numbers remain small compared with healthcare and tech.
- solar photovoltaic installers and wind turbine technicians are high-percentage growth roles tied to new capacity and maintenance.
- these stocks remain more policy-sensitive and cyclical than the healthcare or cybersecurity names.
- stocks for renewables overall: $TSLA
- stocks for solar: $ENPH, $SEDG, $FSLR, $RUN, $NOVA
- stocks for wind / clean energy: $GEV

Industrials:
- industrial machinery mechanics is the main non-service name on the list.
- demand comes from keeping factories, production lines, and equipment running as manufacturing and automation continue.
- this is the more traditional industrial maintenance and equipment side of the economy.
- stocks: $SPCX, $CAT, $DE, $HON, $ETN, $ROK, $EMR

Okay so what to do?
In my opinion, healthcare is the biggest and most durable demand engine on this list. An aging population, doctor shortages, and the move toward more care at home and outside hospitals are already locked in. This is the part of the market that should act as the ballast in a portfolio. Have some real exposure to healthcare.

Tech (data, AI, and cybersecurity) is the higher-upside layer sitting on top of that. These names tend to move more when the market is in growth mode, but they also carry more volatility. So make sure you have exposure here in tech as well for the next decade to outperform the markets.

Renewables and industrial machinery show up on the list, but they are smaller pieces of the overall picture and more sensitive to policy and the economic cycle. It's okay to have crazy volatile names, but make sure you size it correctly.

Whatever happens, let's continue to compound our winners and make millions together.
