AbCellera’s access to a top tier talent pool in their own backyard is an underappreciated asset that doesn’t show up on the balance sheet.

This passage from Zero to One is over 10 years old, but I believe it is still applicable today.

“Researchers experiment with things that just might work instead of refining definite theories about how the body's systems operate. Biologists say they need to work this way because the underlying biology is hard. According to them, IT startups work because we created computers ourselves and designed them to reliably obey our commands. Biotech is difficult because we didn't design our bodies, and the more we learn about them, the more complex they turn out to be.”

As it pertains to “instead of refining definite theories about how the body’s systems operate”, I am reminded of how the properties of a CD3 binding arm can change depending of the tumor binding arm it’s paired with in a bispecific. Mother Nature is indefinite and you must have the tools to navigate that. Of course, AbCellera seems to have proved they can with their diverse panels of binders and matrices used to define their panels of CD3 binders and optimal pairing: x.com/jackprescottx/…

Now, going after biology that has already been validated in some ways, such as NK3R with ABCL635 is an effective way to not be completely in the dark. Indefinite and definite is a spectrum IMO.

But at the end of the day, doing good science is paramount to success. You need elite minds with good judgement to know what targets are worth pursuing, how the studies should be designed, etc.

“experiment with things that just might work”… You need people with the intuition to know what might just work.

See Booth’s comments in the quoted post below regarding the talent pool AbCellera has access to. Again, this is an under appreciated asset.
