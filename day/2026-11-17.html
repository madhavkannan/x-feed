Well, most of the time projections are noise and reactionary.

Like right now, sky-high projections for $ANT $700-$800B ARR over next few years is stirring up hype that I'd argue gets revised down later.

I do think it's important to model in capex forecasts dropping past 2028, due to the current reflexive nature of AI selloffs, rate hikes, etc.

The drawn out duration of Iran War doesn't help, Jensen leaving $NVDA certainty draws concerns over Nvidia's ecosystem.

But any catalyst, like if Iran War ends, can also change sentiment in a heartbeat (since bearish positioning is overwhelming).

- It's also clear financing has been tightening, since OpenAI only managed to raise $11B of their original $30B target so far.

- Markets are reacting enthusiastically to companies like $TE doing a $100M ATM today (nuking valuations)

- Although the "guppy" sector is probably the most sensitive to financing + rate changes.

On bright side... Neoclouds like $NBIS or $IREN seem to be adjusting by getting 100% capex prepayments or 90% GPU prepayments from their counterparties.

What I'd argue though is lower aggregate capex does not apply uniformly in supply chains.

- Mid-high range lasers for NPO/CPO are still sold out, as seen with $LITE now starting negotiations for 2030 capacity.
- MLCCs are seeing further tightening as content usage goes up for AI servers
- ABF substrates to InP substrates not really going anywhere.

If content per system goes up 10X-15X, capacity expands at 2-3X Y/Y, and capex drops 28%.

And content is so cheap that you can triple order and screw over competitors with wafer supply...

I'm pretty confident you'll see acceleration upstream in bottlenecks from optical components to substrates despite potential aggregate capex dropping.

Another rant is:

-I keep seeing this argument that "low P/E is a warning for cyclical that's going to crash" but these are just clown takes

Nobody seems to analyze duration scenarios like capacity expansion vs. content changes vs. demand. to actually see how long those earnings last.

If everyone seems to be exiting DDR2 lines, there's probably going be more tightness in 2027 despite consume demand weakening, even if it looks like it's low P/E.

- Also for concerns about CPO "delays", the same components like lasers are used for NPO. You'll still see 150-200mW NPO lasers or ELS NPO using the same 300–400+ mW lasers as CPO.

We'll just see acceleration with NPO as a bridge if CPO gets delayed and higher range lasers likely to remain in a severe shortage.

- Feels like markets have also completely discredited the merit of take/pay price floor LTAs as well that companies like SK Hynix to Samsung have.

If contractual agreements hold up, memory players look very undervalued.

TLDR:
- I think it's important to model in capex decreasing to numbers like -30% in hypothetical downside sceanrios.

Given AI profitability, it's clearly not sustainable for hyperscalers to aggregately increase capex $500B or $1T+ Y/Y, and that's never been a case I've argued.

- This is also not the same thing as demand destruction that markets are overreacting to now (which haven't happened yet).

- We can see demand revisions for GPUs, ASICs, and heavier cost parts of the supply chain, while acceleration increases elsewhere.

The nuances market miss by indiscriminately selling everything is going to present compelling opportunities when the dust settles. I think in the end institutions are probably going to end up owning more of the same names retail discovered early on.

Anyway, I think it's a good idea to try and survive until more earnings comes out.
