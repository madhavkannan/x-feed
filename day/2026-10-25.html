Even the best companies can experience massive drawdowns.

Here’s why POSITION SIZING can make or break your portfolio.

You can identify a great company, have a strong investment thesis, and even get the technical setup right, but poor position sizing can still destroy your portfolio.

Here’s something every trader and investor should understand:
- 10% position falling 30% costs your portfolio 3%.
- 25% position falling 30% costs your portfolio 7.5%.
- 50% position falling 30% costs your portfolio 15%.

Same stock. Same decline. Completely different consequences.

A few lessons I’ve learned over the years:
- Never let conviction turn into overconfidence. Even your highest-conviction ideas can fail.
- Size positions based on risk, not excitement. Smaller, more volatile companies generally deserve smaller allocations.
- Protect your ability to stay in the game. A 50% portfolio drawdown requires a 100% gain just to recover.

Remember, the goal isn’t to make the most money from a single stock.

The goal is to compound your capital consistently over many years without allowing one mistake to wipe out years of hard work.

You don’t need to be right on every trade or investment to become successful.

But you absolutely need to survive the times when you’re wrong.

Position sizing is what keeps you in the game.

7. Dingxin Optoelectronics - Targeting 15M/year blended (with 70/100mW CW and 100G EML lines)

Obviously mapped many others like Everbright/Accelink and did some guesses around laser mixes, but only included public disclosures.

- 70mW/100mW lasers are actually a bottleneck right now per Innolight/Cambridge channel checks, laser ASPs actually increasing.

Lasers are tighter than they were a few months ago.

I don't think there's a giant moat here, Chinese players are already competitive. But a bottleneck is a bottleneck, and anyone with capacity to monetize that will benefit, since it's a volume game.

In terms of what the larger pluggable makers are actually doing:

- Innolight September disclosure: actively introducing new suppliers and are planning to sign 2-3 year-long contracts to lock upstream capacity for lasers (still actively securing supply for 1.6T pluggables)

So there's some game theory over who can lock up qualified capacity among all your players.

Even if there's some over-ordering, laser BOM is cheap, so they'd probably want to lock up capacity with more suppliers than lose billions in downstream revenue. (also kinda hurts competitors)

- High end lines (for CPO), non-competitive with Western players like $LITE / $COHR as of next few years. But Chinese players would eventually develop the technology.

- $AAOI CEO: Chinese suppliers several years behind on high-power CPO/DWDM laser production "not in the next 2,3 years"

Legitimate competition coming from China, but as of now there's a scramble for CW LTAs among the industry in 2026-2027.
