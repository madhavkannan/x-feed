I believe $NAUT is worth several billion today, because it's on a path to enable us to cure deadly diseases like Alzheimer's with a single prompt by 2027-2028.

The breakthrough is not better protein measurement.

It is reading the state machine of human biology.

In Nautilus' tau paper, they found that phosphorylation is not random.

pT181 makes pT217 about 8x more likely to appear on the same intact tau molecule.

That matters because adding PO4 groups changes tau's charge distribution, which changes its conformation, which changes how tau binds microtubules.

Microtubules are the rails neurons use to move mitochondria, vesicles and proteins over enormous distances.

Tau helps stabilize those rails.

Change tau's molecular configuration enough and:

tau detaches → microtubules destabilize → transport breaks → neurons degenerate

Alzheimer's may therefore not be "too much phosphorylated tau."

It may be a progression through specific molecular states:

tau A → pT181 → pT217 → additional PTMs → pathological proteoform

And until now we largely could not read that combinatorial layer cleanly.

Mass spectrometry measures mass and charge extremely well, but two intact tau molecules can have the same mass while carrying the same phosphate at different positions.

Same atoms.
Same mass.
Very different biological behavior.

Fragmentation can help, but reconstructing the exact PTM configuration of millions of intact molecules becomes difficult.

Nautilus instead repeatedly interrogates the same intact protein with different probes.

Its tau assay currently reads isoform plus 7 phosphorylation sites, creating hundreds of potential molecular states, while reporting about 5.5% end to end CV versus about 25% cited by management as an industry benchmark.

And here is where this gets insane.

Biology does not appear to occupy every theoretically possible state.

Of 768 tau proteoforms their assay could resolve, they observed only 130 across all samples, and far fewer within individual biological systems.

That means the real state space may be highly constrained.

Now collect enough longitudinal data:

initial proteomic state + treatment → final proteomic state

Do that across millions of patients and interventions and AI stops being merely descriptive.

It becomes predictive.
Given molecular state X, what intervention moves the system toward healthy state Y?

That is effectively a biological key:lock search problem.

For tau alone, that could mean finding the intervention that prevents the proteoform trajectory that causes tau to abandon and damage the neuronal microtubule system.

But tau is just one vertical assay.

Add alpha synuclein.
Amyloid beta.
Cancer proteins.
Immune proteins.
Metabolic proteins.

Thousands more.

Eventually:

genome + proteoform state + treatment history → full body molecular state transition model

A genuine digital twin of human biology.

The endgame is not simply detecting disease earlier.
It is asking:

given this person's current molecular state, what intervention moves them back toward the healthy state?

And having an AI trained on billions of measured biological transitions produce the answer.

That is why I think $NAUT is building something much larger than a proteomics machine.

It is building the data layer required for AI to learn how to steer human biology.

Want to know how to avoid major drawdowns and improve your returns in the stock market?

Understanding Stan Weinstein's Stage Analysis is a great place to start. Every stock moves through 4 stages!

One of the most important lessons I've learned from Stan Weinstein's Stage Analysis is understanding where a stock stands in its market cycle and when to buy, hold, reduce exposure, or stay away.

The framework primarily uses the 30-week moving average (roughly equivalent to the 150-day SMA), price action, volume, and relative strength.

Stage 1: Basing / Accumulation

What does it look like?
- Usually forms after a prolonged Stage 4 downtrend
- Stock stops making consistent lower lows and starts trading sideways
- 30-week moving average begins to flatten
- Price moves above and below the flattening moving average
- Selling pressure decreases, and volume often dries up

What does it mean?
- The stock is trying to establish a bottom, but buyers haven't taken full control yet. This is where I start building my watchlist and looking for improving technicals, higher lows, and signs of accumulation.

- Remember, a stock falling 50-70% doesn't automatically make it a Stage 1 base. It needs time to stabilize and build a proper structure.

Stage 2: Advancing/Uptrend

What does it look like?
- Stock breaks out above its Stage 1 base
- Breakout ideally happens on expanding volume
- 30-week moving average starts sloping upward
- Higher highs and higher lows
- Relative strength improves against the broader market

What does it mean?
- Buyers are taking control, and institutional accumulation may be supporting the uptrend.

- This is my favorite stage to own growth stocks! I generally look for Stage 1 → Stage 2 breakouts and constructive pullbacks to rising moving averages.
- The goal isn't to buy at the absolute bottom but to participate when the stock starts showing evidence of a sustainable uptrend.

Stage 3 vs. Stage 4 - This is where a lot of us make mistakes!

Stage 3: Topping / Distribution

What does it look like?
- Usually forms after a prolonged Stage 2 advance
- Momentum slows, and price starts moving sideways
- 30-week moving average begins flattening
- Price repeatedly crosses above and below the moving average
- Breakouts start failing, and selling volume may increase
- Major support levels are still holding

What does it mean?
- The previous uptrend is losing momentum, and the stock may be going through distribution.

- Stage 3 doesn't automatically mean a crash is coming. Some stocks consolidate and resume their uptrends.

- For me, this is where I become cautious, tighten risk management, consider trimming positions, and avoid aggressively adding exposure.

Stage 4: Declining / Downtrend

What does it look like?
- Stock breaks below major support or its Stage 3 range
- 30-week moving average starts sloping downward
- Price trades below the declining moving average
- Lower highs and lower lows
- Short-term rallies often fail near declining moving averages

What does it mean?
- Sellers are in control, and the stock is in a downtrend.

- One of the biggest lessons I've learned is that technicals often reveal weakness before fundamentals do.

- A company can continue reporting strong earnings while its stock is entering Stage 4 because the market is already pricing in future expectations.

The biggest difference between Stage 3 and Stage 4:

Stage 3 = Sideways price action, flattening moving averages, potential distribution.
Stage 4 = Support breakdown, declining moving averages, established downtrend.

Another important thing to understand is that Stage 1 and Stage 3 can look very similar!

Both involve sideways price action and flattening moving averages. The difference is what happened before:

- Sideways consolidation after a prolonged decline could be Stage 1.
- Sideways consolidation after a major advance could be Stage 3.

My approach is simple:
Stage 1 - Build your watchlist and prepare.
Stage 2 - Look for opportunities to participate.
Stage 3 - Protect your gains and manage risk.
Stage 4 - Protect your capital and avoid weak downtrends.

Stage Analysis isn't perfect, but it's one of the most valuable frameworks I've learned in my investing and trading journey.

I use technicals to understand WHEN to participate and fundamentals to understand WHAT I'm buying.

Understanding these four stages can make a tremendous difference in how you navigate market cycles!

Use /requirements-reconcile before you read long AI PRDs

If you get a long AI-written PRD or business requirements document, do not start by reading every page.

In long AI documents, requirements often contradict each other. For example, one page says users can delete records. Another page says records are never deleted. Both pages read well on their own, but they cannot both be true.

To deal with this, run /requirements-reconcile before you review anything long.

It will:

- scan the document
- find contradictions and overlaps
- give you one clear list of requirements that are in, out, or changed

Send that list back instead of 90 pages of comments.
