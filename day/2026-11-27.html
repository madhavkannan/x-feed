I think 2026 has been one of the most challenging years to navigate equities over the past four years.

Let's walk through how this year started and all of the things one needed to do in order to actually participate in the bull market...

To start off, I think 2026 was fundamentally different from 2023, 2024, and 2025 because of sector rotation and long/short pair trades across different sectors.

Over the past 3 years, everyone lost money together across all industries when the markets went down and everyone made money when everything went up. The trend was quite literally your friend and if you followed it short, you won, if you followed it long, you won.

2026 was different.

First, in January you had to basically get two trades right to really benefit: long oil and long metals. We saw gold and silver breakout of a 15 year base and we saw a Venezuela operation that made oil the most important commodity trade of the year. However, you then had to sell commodities into a blow off top by the end of January. If you didn't, you had a 40% drawdown waiting for you.

As we got into the end of February, we saw the administration take us into a war. No one knew how long it really would last for as it was pitched as a short, special operation. It ended up taking the S&P down to 6200 and growth stocks were down 30-40%. Once again, you had to really be long oil or short the $QQQ to seriously benefit off of this.

So, let's say you bought the dip coming out of March and into April when the first ceasefire was announced. We essentially got the most aggressive, leveraged rally we had seen in a decade from March to June...but only in semis.

If you didn't own the semis, you really didn't benefit. You also couldn't just own anything in $SMH, you had to own very high-beta semiconductor stocks that were the definition of picks and shovels, essentially the bottleneck bros trade. Owning $NVDA was not going to carry your year even though it was the most important semiconductor company. Now, you could have also participated in some other sectors, like space with names like $RKLB $RDW $ASTS, but you had to be aggressively in something within the most high-beta growth sectors for the market to feel comfortable giving you a premium.

If you owned anything in software, you had to suffer 50-60% declines across some of the most incredible companies in the space like $PLTR, $SHOP, and $MSFT because the narrative became that Anthropic would kill software. $IGV got cut by 30%.

Well, then comes July. Now, we finally figured out why software was getting hit as hard as it did. Leopold had made one trade very popular -- long semis, short software. Other hedgefunds had copied it. The problem was, the trade had become so popular and so leveraged that other market participants realized a collapse of the trade could literally wipe out entire funds and the overall sectors. As much money as was made on the upside for the AI trade, the downside had now begun. Leopold's fund blew up but survived, Citadel came in and bought everything at a steep discount, and AI names basically fell 50-60%.

In August, we got a bounce. If the entire downside of the trade was based on a hedgefund getting over levered, then that meant the AI trade was back on since the main event had already taken place. However, things felt different. The excitement around memory had faded. The neoclouds were not seeing the same momentum as they were before. AI names were still doing well, but something obviously fell off. If you did not sell the June highs and chose to hold on, you were basically getting ready to roundtrip the entire gains as we progressed through the year. The space names literally fell 70% as $SPCX marked the top for the market and for the space names.

The reason this is so important is because I believe many people bought most of these AI names in May/June, not early in the year. I am a victim of that as well. The rally was so aggressive that if you didn't have any meaningful semi exposure, you probably ended up buying something that was more rooted in hype even if the fundamentals were strong. I mean some of these names were flat for 25 years and had just seen a massive move up, so obviously there were a ton of people buying into names that likely were too high to begin with.

Towards the end of August, the software trade finally started coming back, along with the hyperscalers. $META began showing off Muse, $MSFT guided to FCF positive, $AMZN had their best operating margins ever. Apple $AAPL was strong all year as a defensive play. The narrative about Claude destroying software went away.

September was flat. Nothing crazy to see.

Then came October. We hit ATHs again, not every name in the AI trade got to their June highs, but many names came back. $NBIS was back to $250, $NVDA hit $240, it felt like the bull market was back in action.

Unfortunately, $CRWV was the reason everything changed. By the middle of October, their bonds began to plummet and yields began to spike. Their unsecured debit was yielding 15%...everyone was confused for why a neocloud with $130B+ in backlog was seeing their debt get sold this aggressively. At the same time, Jensen went to Europe for business and back in the states, you couldn't go a day without some type of datacenter delay, credit headline, backstop issues...basically, the entire market started wondering if the financing of this buildout was possible. The 10 year hit 5.4%, the S&P stayed at all time highs, and AI stocks started to weaken but they didn't give out yet. You could've sold and protected some gains here across the entire market, but if you didn't, November was going to be what really wrecked you.

We enter November with the midterms. Everyone, including myself, though it was going to be a clearing event. Instead, it ended up being an event that showed the market that democrats had such a majority of support because of the concerns around the Iran war (which still hadn't ended) and datacenter backlash, that now what needed to be priced in was the slowdown of the buildout and a potential 2027 peak to capex growth.

This changed everything, and the market really started to feel it. By the middle of November, Jensen was ousted as CEO after reports about affairs with a Chinese spy and the market basically round tripped across the entire AI trade. The only safety during this time was consumer staples like $MCD $TGT $COST, basically companies that didn't have anything to do with AI. We got some horrible inflation reports so the chances for a hike went further up and that continued to put pressure on growth stocks. By last Friday, the S&P hit $689 as the Nasdaq fell 5.5% in a day and we wiped out $7.4T of marketcap within a week.

We still don't know if that was the bottom or if we created a double bottom with Wednesday's price action and maybe we are out of the woods, but most AI names ended up either going negative YTD or meaningfully came down from their peak.

All of this to say, it's been a hard year. I think everyone should have some empathy for everyone this year. If you were heavy on margin, chances are you got wiped out as we saw the KOSPI in South Korea round trip a +100% move on their entire index. If you didn't take profits at the top and just kept buying, even when the fundamentals made sense for a company like $MU when it was already down 30% from the highs, you had to experience another 25% decline. If you went short into the first ceasefire, you got hit hard. If you went long commodities at the end of Jan, you got hit hard. If you even picked SaaS names like $NOW at $80 and saw it go to $150 and didn't sell, you got hit hard because of one earnings. $META was a big winner for so many people as the company got through lawsuits and went from $520 to $780, but then they diluted and the stock crashed. Obviously, if you had a meaningful $NVDA position, you had to see that entire position go to 8x forward earnings because of what happened with Jensen. Crypto...didn't even really get to have a meaningful moment outside of the 60K to 85K pump on $BTC which gave some momentum to names like $MSTR $COIN and $HOOD but then Bitcoin also fell back below 60K and crypto ended up falling with tech stocks.

I am not sure if 2027 is going to be this challenging or because we went through this year, maybe next year is going to be similar to one direction across all sectors.

What I do know is volatility is likely not going to end as this market has more and more narratives being part of the story daily with the Fed, AI's impact across the economy, geopolitics, and more. My fundamental belief is being long in exceptional companies that can compound earnings over the long term will likely outperform most strategies and there should be risk mitigation through trying to avoid the really excited moments or really ugly moments by never getting too happy or too sad. There's always opportunities to trim and opportunities to buy the dip, but being net long in the most important technology revolution we have ever seen is probably a better idea than not.

Finally, I really think we should try to have more empathy for one another because the equity markets are supposed to be hard. We all follow them everyday because we are junkies that are obsessed with this game, but this game isn't easy, and 2026 showed how hard the game can be with all the things we had to navigate. Hoping 2027 is a little easier but even if it isn't, excited to still try to navigate and have a chance at playing the game.
