When to buy the dip?

Buy dips in uptrends. Avoid them in downtrends.

In a Stage 2 uptrend, price makes higher highs and higher lows above a rising 200-day SMA. Pullbacks on lighter volume toward rising moving averages often attract buyers, as those averages can act as support.

In a Stage 4 downtrend, the opposite happens. Price makes lower highs and lower lows, while rallies toward declining moving averages often face selling pressure. What once acted as support can become resistance.

A stock trading below a declining 200-day SMA might look cheap, but cheap can always get cheaper.

Before buying a dip, look for:
- Price above a rising 200-day SMA
- Higher highs and higher lows
- Pullbacks on lighter volume toward rising moving averages
- Signs of buyers stepping in at support

The goal isn’t to buy a stock simply because it’s fallen. It’s to buy strength during temporary weakness.

Buy the pullback in an uptrend, not the breakdown in a downtrend.
