Sharing some of my thoughts on the recent weakness across AI and semiconductor names.

One thing I've always believed is that technicals often tell us about strength or weakness before the fundamentals and headlines catch up.

Looking at the broader semiconductor and memory theme, the recent price action is becoming concerning. I was expecting memory & semi stocks to build the right side of their bases, but looking back, the weakness we saw around June-July may have been an early warning sign.

We had a decent bounce over the last three months, but instead of building stronger bases, several names are now testing or losing their 50-day and 100-day moving averages.

On top of that, $NVDA is showing signs of a failed breakout, while $TSM, $SMTC, $NBIS and $LITE have also seen failed breakout attempts. Meanwhile, $AVGO has slipped below its 200-day SMA.

When stocks spend months consolidating, you want to see accumulation, higher lows, improving relative strength, and eventually a breakout. Instead, we're seeing failed breakout attempts, lower highs, and key moving averages being tested across multiple leaders in SemiConductor sector mainly.

One or two stocks showing weakness is normal, but when several leaders within the same theme start showing similar technical deterioration, that's something worth paying attention to.

A LOT of poor price action across semiconductor and AI names lately.

Does this mean the AI infrastructure cycle is over? Not at all! I still believe AI infrastructure has significant long-term growth potential.

But there's a difference between being bullish on a theme over the next 3-5 years and ignoring deteriorating price action in the short term.

As much as I'm bull at heart, you can't ignore what the market is telling you. Sometimes being patient and selective is just as important as being aggressive when the opportunities are there.

That being said, software, healthcare, crypto, and several other themes are holding up relatively well. Even some international markets, like Brazil, are showing encouraging signs of strength.

Just because one theme is correcting doesn't mean the entire market is falling apart. Capital can rotate from one area to another, and new market leaders can emerge while previous leaders consolidate.

The opportunities haven't disappeared. They're just not necessarily in the same places they were a few months ago!

Gold $GLD is a perfect reminder that even the strongest market leaders eventually break down. And what happened to gold could offer an important lesson for semiconductor stocks.

When gold experienced that massive deleveraging candle on January 30, 2026, it was the first major warning sign. Once that kind of leverage gets flushed out, rebuilding momentum isn't easy.

And when one of the world's most important commodities loses its 200-day SMA after a historic multiyear rally, that's a serious warning that the previous uptrend coming to an end.

Let me explain why.

Once an asset enters a Stage 4 downtrend, nobody knows where the bottom will be. That's exactly why many of us, including myself, suggest avoiding falling knives during Stage 4.

Instead of trying to predict the bottom, I prefer waiting for the price to stop declining and begin moving sideways, which is how a Stage 1 base starts developing.

Since my earlier post in June, gold has experienced further selling pressure and down 15% from June. I think we could eventually find support near the 200-week SMA and potentially spend years building a new Stage 1 base.

Look at what happened between 2011 and 2016. Gold experienced a roughly 45% drawdown and took years to establish a meaningful base.

I'm not suggesting we'll see the exact same timeline, but I wouldn't be surprised if gold goes through a similar process of consolidation before its next major uptrend.

And for the record, I'm still a long-term gold bull!

Now, here's why I'm bringing up gold in the first place.

I'm seeing some similarities in the semiconductor ETF $SMH.

$SMH has already declined roughly 33% from its record closing high and experienced significant selling pressure.

When a major index or ETF experiences an extraordinary run over three to four years, it's not unusual for that advance to be followed by a significant drawdown and a prolonged period of consolidation.

Sometimes these corrections last months. Sometimes they take years. That's one of the reasons I place so much importance on the 200-day moving average. It helps me identify whether the longer-term trend is still intact.

Now, before someone says gold and semiconductors are completely different assets, I agree!

Semiconductor companies generate earnings, have different fundamentals, and operate in a completely different industry.

But that's not the comparison I'm making.

I'm comparing how human emotions, institutional positioning, and market cycles show up in price charts.
Technical analysis is essentially the study of market participants' behavior through price and volume. Fear, greed, leverage, accumulation, distribution, and expectations all influence price action.

And those patterns can appear across different asset classes.

Another interesting observation is the 200-week SMA.

I was charting $NVDA and $GOOGL today, looking at how they behaved during previous major market corrections.

Consider some of the historical periods:
- 2018 market correction
- 2020 COVID crash
- 2022 tech bear market
- April 2025 tariff-related selloff

The 200-week SMA has acted as an important long-term support reference during several major drawdowns.

That doesn't mean every stock will reach it, or that it will always hold.

I'm certainly not predicting that $SMH, $NVDA, or $GOOGL must decline to their 200-week moving averages.

I'm simply highlighting an important historical reference that investors should keep in mind when evaluating potential downside risk.

And here's the bigger lesson.

Right now, everyone seems to be waiting for an oversold bounce, a V-shaped recovery, or the exact market bottom.

But markets don't recover just because we want them to. A new uptrend begins when selling pressure eases, buyers regain control, and price action starts confirming improving conditions.

Until then, patience, discipline, and following your own investment & Trading framework matter far more than predicting the next move.

You don't need to catch the exact bottom to make money in the markets.

And you certainly don't need to call a bottom every single day just so you can eventually claim you were right.

You don't need to catch the bottom to build wealth. You need the discipline to survive the downtrend and the patience to participate in the next uptrend.

Want to study how major leaders top out?

Study the $GLD chart.

• After forming a massive 12-year cup & handle, Gold broke out in January 2024 and rallied nearly +170%. This is exactly why I pay close attention to big, multi-year bases.

• I call this framework "4 Bases & 4 Runs." After breaking out of multi-year or multi-decade bases, many leaders tend to advance in a series of bases followed by powerful runs. I've seen similar behavior in names like $GLD, $SLV, and even $MSTR in 2024.

• During these uptrends, leaders often consolidate and backtest key moving averages like the 21-day EMA and 50-day SMA before beginning their next leg higher.

• The real lesson comes at the end. On the fourth run, notice the climactic volume spike - the highest since the initial breakout. The very next day, price gaps down sharply. That's often a sign that liquidity is drying up and the move is becoming exhausted.

It doesn't mean the asset crashes immediately. Dip buyers can still show up. But more often than not, the easy money phase is over, and the stock or commodity enters a much longer period of consolidation before its next major cycle begins.

Study how leaders begin. Study how they end.

The exits are often hidden in plain sight.
