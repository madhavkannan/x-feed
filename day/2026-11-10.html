The Data Center Buildout Is Not Dead

The Senate’s 73-25 vote to advance the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act undercuts the claim that rapid data center expansion is politically finished.

With White House support and President Trump publicly backing the measure as a way to unleash energy production and protect ratepayers, the bill both raises costs for operators and clears the largest practical obstacle to new capacity: the inability to build power and transmission on a workable timeline.

Under the legislation, large loads of roughly 20 megawatts or more must cover the full incremental cost of the generation, transmission, and distribution upgrades built to serve them. Ratepayers are shielded from cost shifting, and states gain additional tools to impose collateral or other requirements. Modeling suggests this could meaningfully increase the retail power price faced by data centers and add roughly 10 percent to the annualized cost of a gigawatt-scale facility. Those numbers are real, and some marginal projects will be repriced or delayed.
